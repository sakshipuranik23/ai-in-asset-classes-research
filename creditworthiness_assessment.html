⚖ Regulatory Design Principle — Why EBMs Are Legally Compliant by Construction
The Monotonicity Constraint: What Black-Box Models Cannot Guarantee
Regulators require that credit models be monotonic — if a company's debt increases, its risk score must go up, not down. If cash flow falls, risk must rise. This is not a preference; it is a legal requirement for regulated credit scoring in most jurisdictions. Complex neural networks frequently violate monotonicity — they find non-intuitive patterns in training data that can produce the wrong direction of effect for specific feature combinations. EBMs solve this by construction: each feature's contribution is modelled as an independent additive function that can be explicitly constrained to be non-decreasing. The monotonicity constraint is enforced during training, not checked after. A regulator auditing an EBM-based credit score can verify monotonicity mathematically — something that is not possible with a neural network without exhaustive testing across all possible inputs.

Research Notes

⚠ Critical Risk — The Adverse Selection Doom Loop
When the Downgrade Causes the Default It Predicted
The Adverse Selection Loop has a precise doom loop mechanism: the AI downgrades an issuer's credit score. Institutional clients on the platform simultaneously reduce exposure. The bond yield rises as demand falls. The issuer's refinancing cost increases because their existing bonds now trade at a higher yield — making their next debt issuance more expensive. Higher debt service costs stress cash flow. Cash flow stress triggers further AI downgrade. The model has created the deterioration it predicted. This is not hypothetical — rating agencies were accused of exactly this mechanism during the 2008 financial crisis, where mass mortgage downgrades triggered forced selling that collapsed the very securities being downgraded. The mitigations are: staggered score publication (not all clients receive the updated score simultaneously), minimum score validity windows (a score cannot change by more than one notch within a 48-hour period without human review), and position-size caps on automatic actions triggered by score changes — requiring human approval for large portfolio adjustments that could move the market.
The Lagging Ledger — Alternative Data Nowcasting
The problemQuarterly financial filings are already 45–90 days old when published. A company that stopped paying suppliers in January will not report the cash flow deterioration until the Q1 filing in April or May — long after the signal was already visible in alternative data.
Job posting signalCompanies under financial stress freeze hiring before they miss a coupon payment — typically 2–3 months earlier. A sudden collapse in job posting volume for a specific company is a leading indicator of financial deterioration that precedes formal reporting by quarters.
Shipping trafficSatellite imagery of logistics activity — container volumes at company warehouses, truck traffic at manufacturing sites — reveals real-time operational activity that predicts revenue before it is reported.
Legal filingsCourt records showing supplier litigation, regulatory filings showing environmental violations, and patent application rates are all publicly available real-time signals that precede financial deterioration by months.
IntegrationThese alternative signals are normalised into a composite "Alt Data Score" that enters the EBM as an additional feature — visible as a separate bar in the feature contribution chart, with its own explicit contribution to the final score.
Why EBMs — Technical Edge
Accuracy parityEBMs often match the performance of Random Forests and Gradient Boosted Trees on tabular financial data — they are Generalised Additive Models (GAMs) that sacrifice almost no predictive accuracy relative to black-box alternatives.
Feature independenceEach feature's contribution is modelled independently and can be visualised as a simple shape function graph — interest coverage on the x-axis, risk contribution on the y-axis. A regulator can audit each feature separately.
Interaction detectionUnlike simple linear models, EBMs find "couplings" — debt is only a risk if cash flow is also low. High debt combined with high cash flow is a very different situation from high debt combined with thin cash flow. The interaction term is explicit and interpretable.
Monotonicity by designMonotonicity constraints are enforced during EBM training — not checked after. This is the critical regulatory advantage over neural networks, which can only be tested for monotonicity empirically after training, not guaranteed before.
