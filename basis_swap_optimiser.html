⚖ Regulatory Design Principle — The Hard Compliance Layer
IFRS 9 / ASC 815: Why the Optimiser Cannot Simply Minimise Cost
Under IFRS 9 (international) and US GAAP ASC 815 (domestic), corporate hedges must maintain hedge effectiveness — a documented, ongoing relationship between the hedging instrument and the hedged item. Constantly modifying or restructuring swap contracts can inadvertently break this effectiveness designation. When a hedge is deemed ineffective, volatility must be recognised immediately on the corporate income statement — potentially creating earnings volatility far larger than the basis spread savings being pursued. The AI optimisation agent is therefore constrained by a Hard Regulatory Compliance Layer that computes the exact impact on hedge accounting status before proposing any trade. Every switch option shown to the treasury committee has already been filtered: if a cost saving would trigger an IFRS 9 break, it is silently removed from the recommendation set. The CFO sees only compliant options.

Research Notes

⚠ Model Risk — RL in a Constrained Multi-Objective Environment
Constrained Reinforcement Learning Is a Research Frontier for This Application
The optimisation objective here is multi-dimensional: minimise basis cost, maintain IFRS 9 effectiveness, respect counterparty credit limits, stay within corporate treasury policy, and preserve subsidiary legal framework compliance. Reinforcement Learning with this many simultaneous hard constraints is genuinely difficult to train. The reward function must encode all constraints simultaneously, and RL is notoriously unstable when constraint violations carry asymmetric penalties — an IFRS 9 hedge accounting break is catastrophic to the income statement; a 5bps suboptimal basis spread is merely disappointing. The white paper should position constrained multi-objective RL as the right algorithmic direction while acknowledging it is still a research frontier for this specific constraint profile. In production deployments today, the optimisation layer is more likely to be a constrained integer programme or a rules-based Monte Carlo simulation with RL as the forward-looking research direction rather than the current implementation.
The OTC Data Opacity Problem
The problemCross-currency basis swap data for longer tenors — 5-year to 10-year USD/INR or USD/BRL — is highly opaque. Outside major market makers, these tenors are infrequently quoted, making it impossible to observe a fair market price for the corporation's existing hedge.
GP interpolationGaussian Process regression uses liquid short-term FX forward points and Cross-Currency Triparty Repo volumes as anchor inputs — fitting a smooth curve through observable data points and interpolating implied longer-tenor basis spreads with calibrated uncertainty bounds.
Why GP specificallyGaussian Processes produce not just a point estimate but a full probability distribution at each interpolated point — the same uncertainty quantification property that makes them valuable in the Yield Curve Modelling use case. A corporation can see not just the implied 5-year USD/INR basis but how confident the model is in that estimate.
Dollar scarcity signalBasis spread widening — particularly USD becoming scarce relative to other currencies — is a leading indicator of global liquidity stress. The Basis Swap Optimiser is therefore also a macro stress early warning signal, not just a cost reduction tool.
