Last Look Latency Arbitrage Sentry

In OTC FX, market-making banks hold a legal window — "Last Look" — to reject trades after quoting a price. Originally designed to protect against technical latency disadvantages, it has evolved into a free option for liquidity providers to reject trades whenever the market moves against them. For institutional buyers, repeated rejections act as a flare gun — signalling trade intent, widening spreads, and causing severe slippage.

The Gap
Execution desks treat Last Look rejections as unpredictable — an unavoidable cost of OTC trading. There is no systematic model for which bank will reject under which market conditions, so routing decisions are made on price alone, not fill certainty.
AI Solution
A Predictive Routing Pipeline that treats each bank as an adversarial black box — inferring rejection thresholds through passive probing, scoring fill certainty in real time via LightGBM, and routing each order to the venue with the optimal price-certainty trade-off.
Value Created
Transforms pure data governance into direct margin expansion — turning bank rejections from an unpredictable cost into a quantified, managed variable. Execution slippage reduction translates directly to P&L.
Strategic Framing — The Ultimate Equalizer
"In an OTC environment where the deck is stacked in favour of the market maker, the Last Look Sentry acts as the ultimate equalizer. By treating bank rejections as a predictable risk variable rather than an unavoidable cost of doing business, an institutional desk can systematically lower execution slippage — transforming pure data governance into direct margin expansion."

⚔ Design Principle — The Adversarial Observer
Inferring Bank Rejection Thresholds Without Being Told Them
Liquidity providers do not publish their internal rejection logic or holding window durations — they are commercially sensitive and actively concealed. The Sentry operates as an adversarial observer: it treats each bank as a black box and runs continuous passive probing loops — sending small, low-risk orders across a systematic range of market conditions (varying volatility levels, order sizes, time-of-day, and order book depth combinations) and recording which conditions trigger rejections. Over thousands of observations, a Bayesian rejection model builds for each bank: a live probability function that maps current market state → expected rejection probability for that specific counterparty. No bank cooperation required. No API access to their internal systems. Just a patient, systematic observer learning the rules of a game the bank is simultaneously trying to hide.

Research Notes

⚠ Two Critical Failure Modes
The Arms Race Problem & The Phantom Liquidity Trap
The Arms Race Problem: The Sentry's adversarial observer derives its value from the bank not knowing it exists. The moment a bank's own analytics detect that a specific client is running a rejection-prediction model — identifiable from their unusual small-order probing patterns across diverse market conditions — the bank can deliberately randomise its rejection behaviour. A bank that varies its holding window duration randomly between 10ms and 200ms, or injects artificial noise into its rejection logic, defeats the Bayesian model entirely. The Sentry is in an arms race with the banks it monitors. The only defence is model diversity — using multiple probing strategies simultaneously so no single detectable pattern emerges — and continuous retraining that can adapt to deliberate randomisation faster than the bank can sustain it.

The Phantom Liquidity Trap: Not all quotes that vanish before execution are Last Look rejections. A significant fraction are phantom liquidity — quotes that appear in the order book for 2–5 milliseconds, placed by algorithmic market-makers to signal pricing intent rather than genuine fill availability. Routing toward a bank with a low rejection score but high phantom liquidity rate produces the same slippage outcome as routing toward a high-rejection bank. The Sentry must distinguish between deliberate Last Look rejections (which the model can predict) and phantom quotes (which evaporate before the order even reaches the bank). The Feature Extraction stage specifically calculates a "Quote Persistence Score" — the average duration a bank's quotes survive before withdrawal — and filters phantom-heavy venues independently of the rejection model.
Technical Architecture
FIX ProtocolThe industry standard messaging protocol for financial transactions — FIX/WebSocket feeds provide the raw millisecond-level quote and execution data that feeds the ingestion layer
LightGBMGradient Boosting Machine optimised for low-latency inference — faster than XGBoost on tabular data at small batch sizes, making it the right choice for per-trade scoring where microseconds matter
Bayesian updatingThe rejection model for each bank is updated continuously as new rejection/fill observations arrive — it is not a static model trained once but a live probability distribution that moves with market conditions
Compliance VaultEvery rejection is logged alongside the full market state data — spread, volatility, order size, book depth, timestamp — providing empirical evidence for MiFID II best execution regulatory audits. The same architectural pattern as the Fixed Income Compliance Vault.
Competitor Landscape — Anti-Last-Look Intelligence
1
XTX Markets — the pioneer of zero-last-look liquidity. Built ML clusters capable of quoting without a holding window, forcing buy-side firms to develop predictive defence tools to compete. Their existence validates this use case commercially.
2
Pragma (TradeNeXus) — adaptive logging measures holding times across bank platforms and dynamically penalises venues with asymmetric rejection tendencies. The closest existing competitor to the proposed Sentry architecture.
3
FlexTrade (FlexFX) — real-time TCA models mapping "Market Impact Following Rejection" — showing traders exactly how much a bank's rejection cost them in subsequent slippage. Post-rejection attribution rather than pre-rejection prediction.
4
BestX (State Street) — industry standard for independent post-trade TCA. Statistical analytics break down exact Last Look costs, helping buy-side firms hold liquidity providers accountable. Level 2 in our maturity ladder — the baseline the Sentry significantly improves upon.
