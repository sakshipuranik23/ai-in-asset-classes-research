Maritime Telemetry Bottleneck Nowcaster

Demurrage penalties — the fee charged when a vessel waits beyond its contracted unloading window — cost the global commodities industry billions annually. Congestion at the Panama Canal, Suez, and major grain ports builds 10–14 days in advance in AIS transponder data that the market is not systematically reading. The Nowcaster reads it.

AIS Transponder Data Port Congestion Modelling RL Routing Logic 14-Day Demurrage Prediction ⚠ AIS Spoofing Risk
The Gap
Physical trading houses manage demurrage as a cost of doing business — unpredictable, absorbed after the fact. No systematic model reads the 14-day window of advance signal that exists in AIS data, port congestion metrics, and canal throughput records before a vessel reaches its bottleneck.
AI Solution
Processes AIS shipping transponder data alongside historical port congestion, canal throughput, and weather vectors using RL routing logic — predicting demurrage penalties and transit delays up to 14 days before a vessel reaches its destination bottleneck.
Value Created
14-day advance warning converts demurrage from an unavoidable cost into a manageable, quantified risk — enabling rerouting, contract renegotiation, or financial hedging before the penalty accrues.
Strategic Framing — The Physical Signal Before the Financial Cost
"Ships slow before demurrage accrues. The 14-day window between congestion forming and a vessel arriving is the longest advance warning period of any commodity market signal — longer than most weather forecasts, longer than most crop reports. The Nowcaster is the system that finally reads that window systematically."

Research Notes

⚠ Two Critical Failure Modes
The Panama Drought Trap & The AIS Spoofing Problem
The Panama Drought Trap: The 2023–2024 Panama Canal crisis demonstrated the core risk of this system — a low-water event caused by El Niño drought reduced the canal's maximum draft from 15.2m to 13.4m, forcing dozens of vessels to reduce cargo loads or wait weeks for water levels to recover. No official advance warning was issued. The AIS data showed the congestion forming over 14 days before vessels reached the canal, but no systematic model was reading it. The trap for the Nowcaster is structural events that have no historical precedent in training data — a first-time drought of this severity sits outside the model's learned congestion patterns. The fix is a physical hydrology model that independently forecasts canal water levels from precipitation data, feeding into the congestion model as a hard constraint rather than a learned feature. If the hydrology model predicts draft restrictions below a threshold, the congestion model switches to a crisis regime regardless of historical AIS patterns.

The AIS Spoofing Problem: AIS transponders can be manipulated — vessels transmitting false position data is well-documented, particularly for sanctioned cargo carrying Iranian crude, Russian oil, and North Korean coal. A tanker that appears in the AIS feed to be anchored off Singapore may actually be transferring cargo in international waters off Malaysia. The Nowcaster trained on AIS data will produce incorrect predictions for any vessel whose transponder data is unreliable. The mitigation is multi-source position verification — cross-referencing AIS data against optical satellite imagery (clear weather) and SAR imagery (cloud cover) to detect position anomalies. Vessels where AIS position is inconsistent with satellite-observed position are flagged with a "Low Confidence — Verify" tag and their predictions are marked unreliable rather than propagated into the demurrage model.
Technical Architecture
AIS data sourcesSpire Maritime, exactEarth, and MarineTraffic provide global AIS feeds. Satellite AIS (S-AIS) from low-earth orbit satellites closes coverage gaps in remote ocean areas where terrestrial receivers cannot reach.
Port congestion dataPort authority vessel queuing records, berth occupancy data, and agent reports from major ports. Supplemented by satellite imagery of anchorage areas to count waiting vessels when official queue data is unavailable.
RL routing modelTrained on historical transit data across major bottlenecks — learning how vessel characteristics (draft, beam, cargo weight), port state (queue depth, berth availability), and macro conditions (weather, seasonal traffic) interact to determine actual vs scheduled arrival time.
Charter party termsThe demurrage calculation requires the specific terms of each vessel's charter party — the contracted laytime (free waiting days) and the daily demurrage rate. These are private contracts; the system requires integration with the trading house's contract management system.
